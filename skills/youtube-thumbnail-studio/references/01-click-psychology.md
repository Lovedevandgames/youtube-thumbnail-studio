# Click psychology

## How a viewer decides (1–2 seconds)
1. **Scroll-stopper** — something in the thumbnail stops the thumb. If nothing does, the title is never read.
2. **Value hunt in the title** — a desire loop ("will this help me?") or an interest loop ("what happens next?").
3. **Visual confirmation** — the eye returns to the thumbnail to check it backs the title's promise.

So: **thumbnail → title → thumbnail**. The title says WHAT, the thumbnail says the FEELING. Thumbnail text adds to the
title and never repeats it ("How to Write a Killer Script" → thumbnail "basically cheating", not "Script Writing").

## Desire loop (before any design)
- Desire: what the viewer wants (a dream, money, growth, beauty, status).
- Pain: what is in the way.
- Transformation: what the video delivers.
- Curiosity: "if I click, I'll see / learn / be able to…".

## Click drivers — one per concept
| Driver | Looks like | When |
|---|---|---|
| Awe / scale | the hero against something huge (or huge themselves), glow, "a show, not a vlog" | locations, events |
| Story / stakes | "then → now", a childhood photo, years, a dream, the underdog | personal stories |
| Open question | a reaction + a hidden answer (screen turned away, "?") | the video has a reveal |
| Before → after | two halves in hard contrast | transformations, challenges |
| Proof | a real screenshot, object or number, big | results, brand replies |
| Playing with the official | the look of a brand/show poster + an outsider hero inside it | next to trending official content |

## 7 scroll-stoppers — use 3 at most
1. Colour contrast (including against your feed neighbours).
2. A big face with emotion (for small channels emotion beats recognition).
3. A strong graphic that expresses the desire loop.
4. Big text, numbers, money (round numbers are magnets).
5. Arrows / circles — rarely and on purpose (skip for premium brands).
6. Aesthetic cinematic imagery (symmetry, light).
7. Collage — words, icons, numbers around the hero.

## Graphics express the loop in one of 4 ways
end state · process · before → after · pain / anti-state.

## Layouts
- Symmetric — hero in the centre.
- Rule of thirds — hero on one third, object/text on the other.
- A → B split — transformation, comparison.

## Facial emotion
| Emotion | Pull | For |
|---|---|---|
| Surprise / shock | highest | reactions, reveals, discoveries |
| Curiosity | high | tutorials, tips |
| Excitement | high | launches, unboxings, a dream come true |
| Concern | above average | warnings, mistakes |
| Confidence / triumph | medium | expertise, "I did it" |
| Neutral | lowest | only for minimalist brands |

Face rules: 30–50 % of the frame; eyes to camera = connection, eyes on an object = curiosity (the gaze leads to the
text/object); face on one side, object or text on the other; never put text on the face.

## Data
- vidIQ, 500 breakout videos (June 2026): 69 % of thumbnails show a face, 56 % use high contrast, 89 % use at least one;
  median text — 5 words.
- YouTube Test & Compare picks the winner by watch-time share, not clicks: clickbait without truth loses.
